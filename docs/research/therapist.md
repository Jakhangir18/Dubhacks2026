# AI Coach Clinical Brief: Gait and Obstacle Training

Date: 2026-10-17

Quotes next to [n] are verbatim from source n; (S) = secondary source; **Design** = team default, not from a source.

## 1. Session structure

**V-TIME** (treadmill plus screen VR; older fallers incl. Parkinson's) [1][2]
- "three times per week for 6 weeks, with each session lasting about 45 min" [2]; "bouts of walking and rest breaks", first session "ideally 20 minutes of walking time" [1].
- Speed: week 1 "80% of over ground gait speed", week 2 "90%", then "increase by 10% every week"; "1 minute of walking will be added to each of the walking bouts" [1].
- Obstacles: week 1 "every 30 seconds, at low level of difficulty", later more often and "on the more challenged side" [1].
- "If subjects are not successful, the difficulty level will be decreased to the level previously achieved and vice versa" [1].
- Feedback "both during training and as a summary at the end" [2].

**C-Mill** (targets and obstacles projected on the belt, like this product; hip-fracture inpatients) [3]
- "40 minutes per session" with "20 minutes of practice".
- Week 1 plain walking "so that participants will become acquainted"; week 2 targets; week 3 obstacles; week 4 speed changes and games.
- Obstacle difficulty: size and response time, "a few steps ahead [easy] or during the swing phase of an ongoing step [difficult]".

**Obstacle height:** no projected-obstacle protocol found. Lab obstacles "0, 25, 51, or 152 mm"; "For the 25 mm obstacle, mean FC was 64 mm, or approximately three times that used in level gait" [4].

**Warm-up:** not specified in either trial; Otago booklet: "Always begin with a warm up" (S) [5].

**Guideline dose:** falls-prevention exercise "three times or more weekly", progressed "for at least 12 weeks" [6].

## 2. Safety

- **Screening:** PAR-Q+ [7] asks 7 questions, e.g. "Do you lose balance because of dizziness OR have you lost consciousness in the last 12 months?" Any YES: "COMPLETE PAGES 2 AND 3".
- **Trial criteria:** V-TIME required "Able to walk for 5 minutes unassisted", excluded "Unstable medical condition including cardio-vascular instability in the past 6 months" [1]; C-Mill excluded "not being allowed to bear weight on the affected leg" [3].
- **Stop:** "chest pain, dizziness or severe shortness of breath, stop immediately"; joint pain: "stop, check your position and try again" (S) [5].
- **Effort:** on 0–10, moderate is "5 or 6", vigorous "begins at a 7 or 8"; moderate effort "can talk, but not sing" [8]. Borg and heart-rate stop thresholds: not found on an opened page; with no heart-rate sensor, use the 0–10 question and talk test.
- **Support:** V-TIME used "A safety harness" with "no weight support" [1]; C-Mill "handrails permit weight-bearing and allow early training in a safe environment" [3]. A walking pad has neither.
- **Unsupervised:** both trials were staff-run; home users should "consult your GP" (S) [5]. Add (**Design**): emergency stop reachable without looking, clipped safety key, carer-set speed cap, rear-of-deck auto-stop, lock-out after any PAR-Q+ YES.

## 4. Motor-learning feedback

- **Focus:** external "on intended movement effects", internal "on movements of body parts"; external won for retention, "Hedges' g value = 0.583" [13]. After publication-bias correction: retention "g = 0.15", effects "small to nil" [14]. Default to external cues; do not call them proven.
- **Frequency:** "no significant effect of reduced feedback frequency at any time point"; "robust evidence ... is lacking" [15]. A gait RCT with "a faded feedback schedule" improved walking; "Visual feedback may be more likely to create dependence on external cues than auditory feedback" [17].
- **KR vs KP:** "prescriptive KP alone was superior to KR; and KR was superior to descriptive KP"; "can be generalized only to novices" [16].
- **Cue wording (Design):** ≤12 words, one instruction; name a belt target, not a body part; say what to do.

## 5. Claims and regulation

FDA "General Wellness: Policy for Low Risk Devices", issued "January 6, 2026" [18]:
- May include "exercise equipment, audio recordings, video games"; wellness claims include "develop or improve endurance, strength or coordination", "multitasking", "Claims to improve general mobility".
- Not wellness: "A claim to restore a structure or function impaired due to a disease or condition", so coaching injury recovery is likely device territory. Falls are not among its disease examples.
- Not wellness if the interface has "references to specific diseases, clinical conditions, or diagnostic thresholds" or prompts that "recommend or require specific clinical action". Allowed: "evaluation by a healthcare professional may be helpful", without "clinical thresholds".
- Low-risk test: may it "pose a risk to the safety of users"? Ask this of a handrail-free belt.
- A "powered treadmill" for "medical purposes, such as to redevelop muscles or restore motion to joints" is "Class I" [19].
- V-TIME's falls result came from supervised, harnessed clinic training [1][2]; it does not show this game works.

- **Say:** "walking game for balance, coordination and focus"; "practice stepping over obstacles"; "your steps today vs last time"; "a check-in with your clinician may be helpful".
- **Avoid:** "treats", "rehab", "therapy", "prevents falls", "reduces fall risk", "clinically proven", "fall-risk score", "abnormal", "below 0.8 m/s", naming a disease or injury.

## 6. Coach specification

**Defaults** (**Design** unless cited)

| Parameter | Default |
|---|---|
| Pre-check | PAR-Q+ [7]; any YES: no unsupervised session |
| Warm-up | 2 min walking, no targets [3] |
| Start speed | 80% of overground gait speed [1]; unknown: pad minimum, raise until "talk, but not sing" [8] |
| Session | 3 bouts × 5 min, 1 min rest; build toward 20 min walking [1]. Demo: one 3-min bout |
| Obstacles | 1 every 30 s [1], shown several steps ahead [3] |
| Lift threshold | Start 5 cm, range 5–15 cm (bounded by [4]) |
| Order | targets, obstacles, questions, then late obstacles [3] |
| Progression | One parameter at a time; 8 of last 10 succeed: up one level; 4 of 10 fail: back one level [1]. Speed only between sessions, ≤+10% a week [1] |
| Feedback | Sound per target or obstacle (KR); one prescriptive spoken cue per 20–30 s; summary at bout end [2] |
| Effort | Ask "Effort, zero to ten?" each bout; aim 5–6 [8] |

**Situations** (triggers are **Design**)

| Situation | Trigger | Action | Example cue |
|---|---|---|---|
| Red flag | Chest pain, dizziness, breathlessness, lasting joint pain [5], "stop" | Stop belt, end session, show clinician note | "Stopping now. Keep stepping slowly until the belt stops." |
| Stumble or drift back | Foot in rear quarter of deck | Stop belt | "Stopping. Small steps until the belt stops." |
| High effort | Effort ≥7, or cannot finish a short answer | Speed −10% or end bout; drop questions | "Let's ease off. Comfortable steps, easy breathing." |
| Obstacle success | 8 of last 10 cleared | Up one level | "Nice clearing! The next ones come a little sooner." |
| Obstacle misses | 4 of last 10 below threshold | Back one level | "Step high over the red bar." |
| Left-right asymmetry | Step-length ratio >1.08 for 20 steps [10] | Targets lengthen the short side | "Reach for the left circles and land right on them." |
| Fast, short steps | Cadence +15% and step length −10% vs warm-up | Wider, slower targets | "Long, steady steps. Land on each circle." |
| Fatigue | Step length −10% vs bout start, more misses | End bout, rest 1–2 min | "Great work. Rest a minute, then one more round." |
| Dual-task slowdown | Misses or shorter steps while answering | Easier or no questions | "Just walk for now. Questions come back soon." |

The coach never names a disease, quotes a clinical cut-off, says "abnormal", or gives medical advice [18].
