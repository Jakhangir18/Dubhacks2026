Date: 2026-10-17

Every number is on the linked page. **[secondary]** marks a news page. PubMed records were read through NCBI E-utilities (the PubMed site blocked the fetcher).

## 2. The engagement problem

- **Parents of children with CP:** "non-adherence rates to HEP were moderate to high, ranging from 34% to 79.2%" (systematic review of 13 studies, mostly outside the US). PLOS ONE 2024: https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0305432
- **One study, Saudi Arabia:** "Only 33.9% (N = 37) of the 109 mothers followed the physiotherapist's recommendations". IJERPH 2022: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9517889/
- **ACL surgery patients (US, n = 10, ages 10–18):** "Self-reported HEP compliance was 75%-100% in 40% (4/10), 50%-75% in 40% (4/10), and 25%-50% of prescribed exercises in 20% (2/10)"; in a card sort, "motivation was ranked first". JMIR Serious Games 2021: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34448715/
- **What parents and therapists say (UK, 9 parent–child pairs + 3 physiotherapists):** "Home exercise programmes constitute 50%–80% of therapy; however, adherence to home programmes is often poor"; parents had "difficulty motivating their children ... especially where they found them boring and repetitive"; one father said "Doing the exercises can be a bit of a battle at times"; "Parents, children and physiotherapists unanimously supported gaming to motivate children". Health Expectations 2025: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12740999/
- "While conventional physical therapy is effective, it is not always engaging for children". J Clin Med 2025: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12608441/

**Weak spot:** no large adherence study in adolescent sports rehabilitation was found; the ACL figure comes from 10 patients.

## 3. Evidence that games help

| Intervention | Effect | Quote and caveat | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| VR games, balance in CP | g = 0.29 | "Hedge's g = 0.29; 95% CI 0.10–0.48"; 11 RCTs, 313 children; "should be viewed with caution owing to current methodological defects" | IJERPH 2019: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6861947/ |
| Non-immersive VR in CP | ES 2.04 (gross motor), 1.34 (balance) | "GMFM-D: ES = 2.04"; "PBS: ES = 1.34"; but "Heterogeneity was high across studies (I 2 > 85% for gross motor function and >93% for balance)". The paper gives 624 participants in the abstract and 437 children in the results. | J Clin Med 2025: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12608441/ |
| CP review of reviews | VR is a weak positive; treadmill training works | "virtual reality serious gaming ... (yellow lights, weak positive)"; "Effective allied health interventions include ... treadmill training, partial body weight support treadmill training" | Curr Neurol Neurosci Rep 2020: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7035308/ |
| Treadmill training in CP | 0.85 endurance, 0.52 speed | "The overall effect size of gait endurance was 0.85"; "gait speed and limb support time were 0.52 and 0.73"; 8 studies, "179 participants"; "difficult to generalize the results owing to the small number of included studies" | J Exerc Rehabil 2020: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7056486/ |
| Motor interventions in DCD | d = 1.06 overall | "The overall effect size (Cohen's d) across intervention studies was large (1.06)"; "Positive benefits were evident for ... active video games"; "the results should be interpreted with caution" | Res Dev Disabil 2018: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29413431/ |
| DCD clinical guideline | games as an add-on | "active video games as adjuncts to more traditional activity-oriented and participation-oriented interventions" | Dev Med Child Neurol 2019: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30671947/ |
| Active video games in developmental disabilities | g = 0.833 | "Hedges' g=0.833" for gross motor skills, "g=0.458" for balance, a "nonsignificant effect for functional mobility"; 12 RCTs, 370 people; "need to be interpreted with caution" | Arch Phys Med Rehabil 2019: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30508504/ |

**Weak spots:** trials are small and mixed, and effects range from g 0.29 to ES 2.04. VR is only "weak positive" for CP and an adjunct for DCD, so pitch the product as a therapist's tool, not a replacement.

## 4. Access gap

- "A workforce shortage exists within pediatric physical therapy (PT) which is likely contributing to limited access to services for children" (no figure given). Pediatr Phys Ther 2025: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40587599/
- All PT, not pediatrics: "There was a national shortfall of 12,070 physical therapist FTEs in 2022"; the forecast says the shortfall is "reaching 8.2% in 2027". APTA: https://www.apta.org/news/2025/03/04/workforce-forecast-2022-2037
- "Therapy deserts" (children's access in one US region; 59 PT clinics answered): "Only 14% of responding PT clinics ... accepted patients with Medicaid-type insurance"; "a median wait of 1 week for PT services". JPOSNA 2024: https://doaj.org/api/articles/16d449e7ab6c4785bfe2768ccc96d3a2
- Children's Hospital Los Angeles, 100 children referred to PT: "Only 27% (11/40) of patients with private insurance experienced a delay as compared to 57% (34/60) of patients with government insurance. Ten percent (6/60) of the patients with public insurance were never able to see a physical therapist." JPOSNA 2023: https://jposna.org/index.php/jposna/article/download/649/872/6264
- **Cost:** no peer-reviewed US per-session price was found (clinic blogs not used). Old CDC figure: lifetime cost "$921,000 for persons with cerebral palsy" (in 2003 dollars). MMWR 2004: https://www.cdc.gov/mmwr/preview/mmwrhtml/mm5303a4.htm

## 5. Pitch slide

1. **1 in 345** US children has cerebral palsy (CDC).
2. **5%–6%** of school-aged children have developmental coordination disorder (CMAJ).
3. **34%–79.2%**: home-exercise non-adherence among parents of children with CP, range across studies (PLOS ONE review).
4. **g = 0.833**: gross-motor gain from active video games, 12 RCTs in developmental disabilities (Arch Phys Med Rehabil; authors urge caution).
5. **14%** of responding PT clinics in one US region accepted children with Medicaid-type insurance (JPOSNA).

**30-second pitch:** One in 345 American children has cerebral palsy, and 5 to 6 percent of school-aged kids have developmental coordination disorder. Much of their therapy is homework, and studies find that 34 to 79.2 percent of families do not stick to it. Games can help: across 12 randomized trials, active video games gave a large gain in gross motor skills, an effect size of 0.833. And help is hard to reach: in one US region, only 14 percent of PT clinics that answered a survey took children on Medicaid. Our AI coach turns the homework into a game and sends the therapist the progress.
